An offsite is a room, a projector and lunch. Two of those are ours.

A Brampton room for 50 to 125, opening in 2027 — team days, AGMs, trainings and holiday parties, priced by the day of the week, never by the head.

Most “corporate event space” is a banquet hall having a quiet week.

Search for corporate event space in Brampton and you mostly find wedding rooms with a screen wheeled in, priced for a Saturday and puzzled by a Tuesday. The person booking is almost never the person giving the speeches — it’s the office manager, the EA, the people-ops team of one. This page is written for her.

What a working day needs is short and specific. Offsite that is actually off site. One conversation at a time, so the back row can hear the front. Chairs still tolerable in hour three. Food the whole staff list can eat. And one invoice that clears finance the first time.

We won’t describe the building; there isn’t one yet. The space search is happening now, and the working-day list rides along to every viewing: wifi that holds under forty laptops, power where people sit, a load-in that doesn’t cross the session. Constraints we’re holding, not specs we can print. The day we sign, we publish what we got.

A team offsite in four hours, hour by hour.

The Corporate Retreat runs Monday to Thursday, four hours, $1,100–$1,500 plus HST, projector, screen and coffee handled. For the walkthrough, call it ten to two.

  • Doors, coffee, the projector test The room is set when you arrive. Plug the laptop in first; make the cable fail at ten past. 10:00
  • The morning session The reason for the offsite goes first, while the coffee is still working. Ninety minutes, one topic, phones face down. 10:30
  • Lunch, hot, from your caterer Your caterer loads in inside the block and serves from labelled trays. Thirty-five minutes is plenty when the food is good. 12:00
  • The afternoon session Shorter, on purpose — the slump is real. End on one slide: who is doing what, by when. The slide people photograph. 12:40
  • Out, cleanly The block ends. Cables and chafers leave with their owners; the cleaning is ours. 2:00

The Short Block-to-Retreat difference is roughly the projector, screen and coffee. Elsewhere: projector and screen $125, premium PA with wireless mics $175, serving staff $40 an hour each.

A forty-person team day: the Retreat at $1,300, lunch from your caterer at $25 a head is $1,000 — call it $2,300 plus HST, and everyone ate.

The usual alternative: a day-delegate rate at $80 a head is $3,200 for the same forty people, and you eat the conference menu.

The paperwork is part of the product: an invoice from The Laurel Room Inc., the 13% HST broken out as its own line, your PO number printed where finance looks for it, a quote carrying the same two-sided band as this page. If procurement needs the quote before the contract, ask — that’s a sentence, not a negotiation.

The holiday party takes the evening block, and the drinks rules are real.

Evenings run 5:30 to 11:00, and for a work party the 11 PM end is a feature — nobody has to decide it’s over. The Space on a Friday-to-Sunday evening is $1,600–$2,200 plus HST; The Styled Experience is $2,800–$3,800 if you’d rather walk into a finished room. A photo booth is $450–$650. With the 13% HST, the room itself comes to $1,808–$2,486 all in — the rest of the budget is dinner and the bar.

If there’s a bar, Ontario law is not interested in your org chart — a staff party needs the permit, the Smart Serve-certified pour and the security hours like any other event, at the rates in the rows beside this. The full walkthrough is on the plan-your-event page; start it a month out.

Who to book, by category. We don’t keep a list, on purpose.

No named vendors — we hold ourselves to procurement rules, and no supplier makes a preferred list before they’ve performed here. The full reasoning is on the outside-catering page.

Caterers, plural if needed
An office is not a family; nobody chose their colleagues by cuisine. One menu rarely covers halal, vegetarian and kosher-style needs plus the serious allergy — so bring two caterers, or one who labels honestly. Ask for an allergen sheet. A working lunch in trays: call it $20 to $30 a head.
Facilitators
Someone to run the agenda so the most senior person can participate instead of chairing. Ask how they handle the afternoon slump, and get the half-day rate in writing.
Headshot photographers
A corner, a plain backdrop, three minutes a person. Ask per-person versus half-day pricing, and when the files arrive — the answer you want is a date.
DJs and hosts, for the party
For the holiday party, not the strategy session. The evening ends at 11 — say so up front, so they plan the last hour backwards. Travelling light, the premium PA with wireless mics is $175 here.
