Five directions, chosen before the walls.

These are the palettes we’re committing to now, in the colours we’ll be buying. Our room opens in 2027, so there is nothing to photograph yet — and we’d rather show you real colour than a render of a room we made up.

Laurel

sage, cream, and something green on every table

Pick Laurel and the lighting we bring comes in soft green. Tables go under sage linen with cream runners, eucalyptus laid down the middle and pillar candles burning between the place settings. The backdrop arch is dressed in the same greenery, so the head table and the arch behind it read as one piece. It’s the quietest of the five — the one to choose when you want the food and the people to be the loudest thing there.

Sage linen, cream runners, eucalyptus, pillar candles.

Golden Hour

amber light, brass, and candles worth the wax

Golden Hour keeps a seven o’clock evening going all night. The lighting arrives amber and stays low, which leaves antique brass candlesticks and tapers to do most of the work down the tables. Linens sit in the warm range underneath them, and the backdrop is dressed to catch the light rather than compete with it. Book it against an evening block, when there’s real dark to push back on.

Amber light, antique brass, taper candles, warm linen.

Blush

dusty pink on cream, rose gold at every seat

Blush runs dusty pink over cream and lands softer than the swatch suggests. Tables get pink linen with cream napkins and a rose-gold setting at every place; the lighting we bring is warm pink, kept low enough that skin tones stay honest in your photographs. Centrepieces and the flowers on the arch hold to the same narrow range, with no second colour anywhere. It’s the direction we’d point you at for a shower or an engagement.

Dusty pink linen, cream napkins, rose-gold place settings, warm pink light.

Monochrome

black, white, and nothing apologising for it

Monochrome takes the colour out on purpose. Black and white linen, silver at each setting, clear acrylic in the centrepieces, and no colour wash in the lighting at all — clean white light and hard edges instead. The backdrop gets stripped back to its structure. Bring a red lehenga or a bright cake into this one and it will be the only colour in the frame, which is the entire idea.

Black and white linen, silver, clear acrylic, white light.

Regal

jewel tones, gold, and the full production

Regal is the heaviest of the five, and it is meant to be. Jewel-tone linen — plum, deep magenta, whichever end of the range you want — with gold chargers and crystal in the centrepieces. The lighting deepens toward the linen colour, and the backdrop is dressed fullest here: flowers top to bottom, drapery behind. Built for a sangeet or a reception, and the direction that most rewards a full-day booking.

Jewel-tone linen, gold chargers, crystal, drapery behind the arch.
