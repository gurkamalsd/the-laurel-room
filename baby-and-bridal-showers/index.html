A baby shower venue in Brampton that isn’t a wedding hall on its day off.

Most rooms a shower can rent exist for something else — the hall for Saturday weddings, the restaurant back room for dinner service. A shower is a daytime event with its own shape.

The shape is specific. Two to three hours in the early afternoon, almost everyone seated, food in trays, not courses. One wall matters more than the rest — backdrop, garland, the guest of honour’s chair — because every photograph is taken against it. And the long stretch in the middle, the gift opening, needs sixty people to see one chair.

Book a banquet hall and you get the rehearsal problem: sixty guests in one corner of a room lit and priced for three hundred. Host it at home and one person cooks, serves, referees the games and sees none of it. The middle option — a room sized between 50 and 125 — is the one Brampton mostly doesn’t have. It’s the room we’re designing, and why it stops at 125 is an argument we’ve written down.

The bridal side runs on the same arithmetic. A bridal shower venue in Mississauga or Brampton usually means a private-room brunch, or a hall treating the afternoon as a small wedding. A bridal shower isn’t a small wedding. It’s its own event, with a dessert table where the head table would be.

The dessert table is the centrepiece. Plan it like one.

A shower has no first dance and no head table. It has one table everybody photographs, and three kinds of people who can build it.

Dessert stylists
They build the table: cake, sweets trays, stands, the styling that makes it one thing. Ask whether they style on site or drop off, when they collect their stands — teardown ends at 3:30 — and what a serving costs. Call it $4 to $6 a piece: $240 to $360 for sixty guests, before the cake.
Grazing tables and brunch caterers
Shower food is eaten in waves. Ask what arrives hot, what holds two hours at room temperature, and who staffs the table. A chafing dish set of four rents from us at $140; serving staff are $40 an hour each. Some families staff their own trays instead.
Balloon and backdrop artists
The wall every photo is taken against. Ask how long the install takes — it has to fit inside a block that opens at 10:00 — and who strikes it. Or have us do it: the garland add-on is $300–$550 by size, custom vinyl on the arch $85.

No names for any of these categories — the reason has its own page: a list we haven’t earned is just a menu of people we like eating with.

“The shower is the celebration most often squeezed into a room that was never meant for it.”
Khushbu Dhahan, founder

From Khushbu

I’ve hosted showers out of living rooms with the sofa pushed to the wall, and I’ve sat at showers where sixty of us filled one corner of a hall built for three hundred. Neither felt right. The living room means the host cooks, serves, runs the games and sees none of it. The hall means paying for a room you don’t fill and feeling small in it.

My career is in government program delivery — operations, budgets, compliance — so I ask where money and effort actually go. At a shower they go three places: the food, the dessert table, and the one wall everyone photographs. The room should serve those three things and get out of the way.

I’m a mother in Brampton. I’ve been the person the shower was for, and the person who threw it. Both jobs are easier in a room the right size. I planned enough of my own family’s celebrations to know that room was the one I could never find, so we’re building it.
