The ceremony is short. The evening around it is why you book a room.

A Brampton room for 50 to 125 guests, opening in 2027. Hold the nikkah and dinner in one evening, or take the whole day with a coordinator. Your caterer, your officiant, your seating plan.

A nikkah asks different things of a room than a reception does.

Start with the signing table, because the day arranges itself around it. The couple, the officiant, the witnesses, the documents, a pen that works and a spare. It needs two things halls forget: everyone should see it, and everyone should hear it — at ninety guests, that means a microphone on the officiant.

Seating is the question families brace to ask, so here is the plain answer. Separate for men and women, fully mixed, or separate for the ceremony and mixed for dinner — all three are normal, and we’re designing the room to move between them without fuss. Nobody here will need the arrangement explained.

Your imam or officiant is yours; there is nothing for us to approve. We ask for a name, a number and an arrival time. Settle one thing early: the Ontario marriage licence comes from a municipal office and is the couple’s job, so ask your officiant who registers what.

The walima keeps its own clock — the same evening or the next weekend, both work here. A walima on its own is a dinner for the people you love, which is exactly what this room is for.

One evening or the whole day. Both timelines, honestly.

The evening block runs 5:30 to 11:00 and holds both without rushing either. The full day runs 10:00 to 11:00 at night, with a coordinator.

The people the day needs, and what to ask them.

We haven’t earned a vendor list and won’t publish one — the reasoning is on the outside catering page. The categories and the questions, we can give you now.

Officiant
Ask how long the ceremony runs, what they need from the room, and whether they’re registered to solemnize marriages in Ontario — that decides whether the legal signing happens at your table or at a civil appointment. Settle it months out.
Caterer
The one vendor who touches every guest. Ask who cooks on the day and how the food travels. A hundred guests at $35 a head is $3,500 — get the per-head quote in writing, staff and rentals as their own lines.
Photographer and videographer
The signing is the photograph the day exists for — small, crowded, quick. Ask to see a nikkah in their portfolio, not just receptions, and price weekday hours against a full Saturday.
Cake and dessert
No cake-cutting fee here, ever. Ask who delivers and when, and which cakes survive a warm evening — the honest ones tell you which can’t.

I’ve spent years planning my own family’s celebrations, and the small weddings were always the hardest to place.
