Built because we kept renting rooms that weren’t ours.

A letter from the founder, three journal entries, and the argument for a Brampton room that seats 50 to 125 with your caterer at the door. Opening 2027 (construction estimate).

typed by the founder, not a copywriter
Document L-001 From the founder Signed · Khushbu Dhahan Aug 2026

Every celebration our family has thrown started the same way: with weeks of searching. Before every mehndi night, every baby shower, every milestone birthday, I went hunting across Brampton for a room that was beautiful, let us bring our own food, and didn’t wreck the budget. Three requirements. I never got all three in one address.

The hunt always ended at the same two doors. A banquet hall built for 300, where eighty guests rattle around and a mandatory in-house menu replaces the food your family actually eats. Or a community centre — folding chairs, fluorescent light, and everyone politely pretending the room doesn’t matter. For 50 to 125 guests, the middle simply didn’t exist here.

By day, I deliver government programs — operations, budgets, compliance. Big programs, hard deadlines, no tolerance for “it fell through.” I’m also a mom, which is its own kind of program delivery. At some point, standing in yet another echoing hall that was wrong for us, I stopped waiting for somebody else to build the room.

So The Laurel Room is the room I couldn’t find. Just over 2,000 square feet under an 18-foot ceiling. Twelve harvest tables that seat 125. Your caterer at the door — any caterer, any cuisine, no minimums. Prices printed in public with the 13% HST named beside them. It is not built yet, and we won’t pretend otherwise: what you’ll find on this site are honest drawings, real prices, and a journal of every decision as we make it.

If you’ve run the same search I have, I’d genuinely like to hear what you’re planning. I answer my own phone.

— Khushbu

Entry 003 Aug 2026

The room on paper.

This month the room became a drawing. A-101 — the floor plan on our homepage — is the version of The Laurel Room I’ve carried in my head for years, finally dimensioned: 50 feet by 40, the statement arch set into the top wall, a kitchenette for warming and plating, step-free entry, seats for 125.

The decisions, on the record. Twelve harvest tables instead of rounds, because long tables seat everyone shoulder to shoulder and no cousin gets stranded at the far table by the speakers. An 18-foot ceiling, because a fairy-light canopy needs air above the dancing — height is the one thing you can’t add later. Five looks — Laurel, Golden Hour, Blush, Monochrome, Regal — and not a catalogue of fifty, because five is the number a real setup crew can execute perfectly, not approximately.

None of it is framed or painted yet, and that’s the point of drawing first: changes are cheap on paper. If you’d put the arch somewhere else, now is the moment to say so.

— Khushbu Dhahan, founder

Entry 002 Aug 2026

Why 125 guests, not 300.

Every hall we ever rented was built for someone else’s wedding. Eighty people in a room for three hundred feels like a rehearsal: empty chairs photograph louder than full ones, and the DJ plays to a dance floor sized for a crowd that was never coming. A hall built for 300 also has to charge like one — mandatory in-house catering, minimums, a menu your family didn’t choose.

So here is the math on tables. Just over 2,000 square feet seats 125 at twelve harvest tables — roughly ten chairs to a table, plus the head table under the arch. All 125 cross-back chairs are seats we expect to fill, because the room is sized to the guest list instead of asking the guest list to grow into the room.

The cap is the promise. If your list runs to 300, Brampton has halls that do that well. If your list is 50 to 125 — the size of nearly every mehndi night, shower, aqiqah, and milestone birthday our own family ever threw — this room is being built to fit it exactly.

— Khushbu Dhahan, founder

Entry 001 Aug 2026

Why we’re building this at all.

Because I got tired of the search. Before every mehndi night, baby shower, and milestone birthday, I lost weeks to the same hunt: a Brampton room that was beautiful, let us bring our own caterer, and didn’t wreck the budget. I never found all three in one address.

My career is in government program delivery — operations, budgets, compliance. Programs that size don’t get to shrug and say it fell through. Eventually it occurred to me that the same skills could deliver the room my own family kept needing. I’m a mom; I know exactly what that room has to do on the day it matters.

So we’re building it: 50 to 125 guests, your caterer at the door, pricing printed in public, opening in 2027 (construction estimate). This journal is where I keep myself honest along the way.

— Khushbu Dhahan, founder
