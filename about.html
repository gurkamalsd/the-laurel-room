About

Built because we needed it.

A venue for 50 to 125 guests didn’t exist in Brampton — the halls were too big, the community centres too grim. This is the story of the room in between.

01The gap

700,000 people. Nowhere to seat eighty.

Brampton is one of Canada’s youngest and fastest-growing cities. More than half the city is South Asian, with big Caribbean, Filipino and East African communities beside it. Families here celebrate constantly — mehndi nights, aqiqahs, showers, milestone birthdays that fill a hall.

But booking a room for those events meant two options: a banquet hall built for 300, with mandatory in-house catering and someone else’s menu, or a community centre with folding chairs and fluorescent tube lighting. The middle — 50 to 125 guests, beautiful, bring your own caterer — simply wasn’t there.

02Raw, then finished

We kept the bones and warmed everything else.

The space is an industrial shell — exposed black ceiling, 18 feet of height, bare concrete. Onto that go warm wood floors, alabaster walls, and a canopy of fairy lights. The height stays. The echo goes.

These are our design renderings — the real room is going up now. Follow the build on Instagram.

03What we run on

Four rules we don’t bend.

  • Every event counts A naming ceremony for sixty gets the same setup care as a wedding for 125. No event is the practice run.
  • Cultural fluency without the pigeonhole We host mehndi nights, christenings, Diwali parties and corporate off-sites without assuming what yours should look like. You tell us; we set the room.
  • BYOC is non-negotiable Bring your own caterer, always. No in-house menu, no food minimums, no surcharge for serving what your family actually eats.
  • Beautiful shouldn’t mean expensive A room worth photographing, from $895. Rates printed on the site, 13% HST named up front, nothing hidden after it.

04The founder

I couldn’t find this room. So I’m building it.

My career is in government program delivery — operations, budgets, compliance. Big programs, hard deadlines, no room for “it fell through.” That’s the training I run this venue on.

But the real reason for The Laurel Room is that I’m a mom. Before every mehndi night, every baby shower, every milestone birthday, I’d spend weeks hunting for a room that was beautiful, let us bring our own food, and didn’t wreck the budget. For 50 to 125 guests in Brampton, that room didn’t exist.

So I’m building it — warm wood under an 18-foot ceiling, five ways to set it, your caterer at the door. If you’ve run the same search I have, tell me what you’re planning. I answer my own phone.
